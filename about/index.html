Sources and licenses

This is the public edition: a static website, free to read. It carries only texts that are in the public domain or openly licensed, and links to the rest.

  • Douay-Rheims Bible, Challoner revision (1749–52), with Challoner's notes, introductions and chapter summaries

    Public domain. Digital text from Project Gutenberg eBook #1581.

  • Clementine Vulgate

    Public domain, from the open-bibles collection.

  • Hebrew and Aramaic

    Westminster Leningrad Codex, public domain. Lemmas and morphology: Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible (morphhb), CC BY 4.0.

  • Greek New Testament

    SBL Greek New Testament (SBLGNT), © 2010 Society of Biblical Literature and Logos Bible Software, CC BY 4.0. Morphology and lemmas: MorphGNT, CC BY-SA 3.0.

  • Byzantine text (verses the critical text omits)

    Robinson–Pierpont Byzantine Majority Text (2018), public domain. Four verses from STEPBible TAGNT: data created by www.STEPBible.org based on work at Tyndale House Cambridge, CC BY 4.0.

  • Strong's dictionaries (1890 and 1894)

    Public domain. Digital edition: Open Scriptures, CC BY-SA. One-word glosses (Tyndale House): TBESH and TBESG, data created by www.STEPBible.org based on work at Tyndale House Cambridge, CC BY 4.0.

  • Catechism of the Catholic Church

    Paragraph numbers only. Each verse lists the paragraphs that cite it, and each number opens that paragraph's page on vatican.va. The text is © Libreria Editrice Vaticana (English translation © United States Conference of Catholic Bishops) and is not reproduced here.

  • Haydock's commentary (1859 edition)

    Public domain, from haydockcommentary.com. Haydock's book prefaces, the 71 chapters missing from that site (Jeremias 4-52, 1 Peter, Psalms 58 and 78-87 and others) and 7 chapters it holds wrongly come from the Haydock Bible on archive.org (Public Domain Mark 1.0), with Haydock's abbreviations for his sources spelled out as on the website (“C.” is Calmet). Where Haydock names a Father or Doctor of the Church as his source, the note is listed under that writer.

  • Church Fathers

    Quotes keyed to each verse by the HistoricalChristianFaith Commentaries-Database (a compilation dedicated to the public domain), taken only from public-domain translations: Schaff's Ante-Nicene and Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers (1885-1900), checked word for word against the Christian Classics Ethereal Library's copies; the Library of the Fathers; other translations published before 1931; and translators' own public-domain releases. Each quote names its translation. Long passages are shortened, marked ….

  • Book introductions

    Summaries in our own words, written with AI help from the sources each line cites, which are linked rather than quoted: the Catholic Encyclopedia (1907-1914; text public domain, read on Wikisource, whose transcriptions are CC BY-SA), Haydock, Challoner, the Council of Trent in J. Waterworth's translation (1848; e-text from the Hanover Historical Texts Project, used for education with thanks), and Church documents on vatican.va. The Pontifical Biblical Commission's answers of 1905-1915 are given as historical notes.

  • Verse numbering

    The Catechism prints modern verse numbers; each citation is matched to the Vulgate and Douay verse with the TVTMS versification table, data created by www.STEPBible.org based on work at Tyndale House Cambridge, CC BY 4.0.

  • Authority badges

    A badge says how firmly the Church has settled one reading of a verse. Only badges a person has approved are shown, and none has been approved yet. What the badges mean.

  • Doctors of the Church

    The list, dates and notes were written for this app; the links go to New Advent and the Christian Classics Ethereal Library.

Changes made

Verses were re-keyed to the Vulgate and Douay numbering, the original words matched to Strong's numbers, and the STEPBible glosses shortened for the interlinear line. The adapted MorphGNT and Strong's data are shared under the same licenses as their sources: CC BY-SA 3.0 for MorphGNT, CC BY-SA for the Open Scriptures Strong's.

Not in this edition

  • The Catechism's text (copyrighted; the paragraph numbers link to it instead).
  • The Septuagint Greek of the deuterocanonical books (the CATSS/CCAT text is licensed for non-commercial personal study only).
  • The Catena Aurea of St. Thomas Aquinas. The 1841–45 translation is in the public domain, but the digital copies this app used are marked copyrighted by the sites that host them.
  • Church Fathers quotes from modern copyrighted translations, machine translations or translations whose rights could not be confirmed; quotes whose wording came through New Advent's revised copy; and quotes whose wording only partly matches the old translation they name.
  • AI-written verse notes.

Use it on a phone

  • iPhone: open the site in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android: open it in Chrome, tap the menu, then Install app (or Add to Home screen).
  • Chapters, verse notes and searches you have opened keep working without a connection.
