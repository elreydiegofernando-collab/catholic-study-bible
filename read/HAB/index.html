Habakkuk

Douay: Habacuc · 3 chapters

At a glance

Author

Habacuc, of whose birthplace, parentage and life the 1913 article says there is no reliable information; legends of various kinds grew up around him. Challoner calls him a native of Bezocher who prophesied in Juda before the Chaldean invasion, and reports the general opinion that he is the Habacuc carried by the angel to Daniel in Babylon (Daniel 14).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Habacuc (Habakkuk)”↗Challoner, Introduction↗

Date

The 1913 article lists many views: the last years of Manasses (the rabbis and many Catholic scholars), the time of Josias, the time of Joakim before or after Carchemish (605 B.C.), and even the Exile (St. Jerome), which it thinks least probable; it favors about 605-600 B.C. with Van Hoonacker.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Habacuc (Habakkuk)”↗

Setting

Juda in its last decades before the Babylonian invasion, when injustice went unpunished and the Chaldeans were rising as a fearsome power.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Habacuc (Habakkuk)”↗Challoner, Introduction↗

About this book

Audience

Juda, and every believer troubled that God seems to allow the wicked to prosper.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Habacuc (Habakkuk)”↗

Purpose

The 1913 article describes a dialogue in which the prophet asks how long the wicked will prosper and God answers that the Chaldeans will come as his scourge, and then that they in turn will be judged; a lyric ode of trust closes the book.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Habacuc (Habakkuk)”↗

Key themes

  • 'How long, O Lord, shall I cry?' The prophet questions God (1:2).
  • The vision that will surely come, though it tarry (2:3).
  • 'The just shall live in his faith' (2:4).
  • Five woes on the oppressor (2:6-20).
  • Joy in God even when the fig tree does not blossom (3:17-18).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Habacuc (Habakkuk)”↗Rom 1:17; Gal 3:11; Heb 10:38

Structure

The 1913 article finds two parts: a dramatic dialogue between God and the prophet (1:2-2:20), ending with a taunting song of five woes; and a lyric ode or psalm (3).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Habacuc (Habakkuk)”↗

How the Church reads it

St. Paul builds on 'the just shall live in his faith' (2:4) in Romans (1:17) and Galatians (3:11), and Hebrews (10:38) cites it too. Haydock (citing Worthington) reads the canticle of chapter 3 as speaking of the appearance of Christ, the last judgment and eternity, and the 1913 article notes that the Greek and Latin Churches keep his feast on 15 January.

In the canon

One of the twelve minor prophets in Trent's list (Session IV) and in the Catechism's (CCC 120); Haydock notes that Sirach already speaks of 'the twelve' as a collection (Ecclesiasticus 49:12 Douay).

The old texts and the sources

Haydock's preface (1859 text)
Habacuc was a native of Bezocher, and prophesied in Juda some time before the invasion of the Chaldeans, which he foretold. He lived to see this prophecy fulfilled, and for many years after, according to the general opinion, which supposes him to be the same that was brought by the angel to Daniel, in Babylon. Dan. xvi. (Challoner) — He might very well live to see the captives return, as only sixty-six years elapsed from the first of Joakim, when he began to prophesy, till that event. He retired at the approach of the Chaldees, and afterwards employed himself in agricultural pursuits. (Calmet) — The sins of Juda, the coming of the Chaldees, and the relaxation of the captivity are specified; and in the canticle, the appearance of Christ, the last judgment and eternity, (Worthington) are mentioned in the most sublime style. (Haydock)
