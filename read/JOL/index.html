Joel

3 chapters

At a glance

Author

Joel son of Phatuel, of whom, the 1913 article says, nothing is known beyond the book; his many references to the Temple, altar and priests suggest he lived in Jerusalem and was perhaps of priestly descent. Challoner gives St. Jerome's explanation of his name, 'the Lord God', or as others say 'the coming down of God'.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Joel”↗Challoner, Introduction↗Haydock (1859), Preface↗

Date

The 1913 article calls the date the most difficult problem in the book: early commentators with St. Jerome put him in the 8th century B.C. with Osee and Amos, others under Josias, and later commentators after the Exile. It concludes that no hypothesis has convincing proof.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Joel”, Date of the Prophecy of Joel↗Haydock (1859), Preface↗

Setting

Juda and Jerusalem during a devastating plague of locusts and a drought, when the Temple offerings had failed.

About this book

Audience

The priests and people of Juda, called to a solemn fast and assembly of repentance.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Joel”↗Challoner, Introduction↗

Purpose

The 1913 article sees the book as a miniature of the prophets' chief themes: warnings of the Lord's judgment to rouse the people, and joyful promises of salvation to keep alive faith in God's kingdom, the judgment being a purification that prepares for the salvation.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Joel”, Contents of Joel↗

Key themes

  • The day of the Lord, announced by a locust plague.
  • 'Rend your hearts, and not your garments' (2:13).
  • The outpouring of God's Spirit on all flesh (2:28-32 Douay).
  • Judgment of the nations in the valley of Josaphat (3).
  • The 'teacher of justice' promised to the people (2:23 Douay).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Joel”, Contents of Joel↗Challoner, Introduction↗CCC 1287↗CCC 1430↗

Structure

Four discourses by the 1913 article's reading: two on the locust plague and the call to repent (1:2-2:17), and two on God's answer, first the end of the plague and the gift of the Spirit, then the judgment of the nations and the blessing of Juda (2:18-3:21).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Joel”, Contents of Joel↗Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Joel”, Literary and Theological Character of Joel↗

How the Church reads it

At Pentecost St. Peter declared that the outpouring of the Spirit fulfilled Joel's prophecy (Acts 2:16-21), and the Catechism teaches the same (CCC 1287, 715). It also uses Joel's call to rend the heart, not the garments, in teaching that conversion is first of the heart (CCC 1430). Challoner reads the 'teacher of justice' as Christ, and Haydock (after St. Jerome) the Spirit's descent as on the hundred and twenty gathered in Sion.

In the canon

One of the twelve minor prophets in Trent's list (Session IV) and in the Catechism's (CCC 120); Haydock notes that Sirach already speaks of 'the twelve' as a collection (Ecclesiasticus 49:12 Douay).

The old texts and the sources

Haydock's preface (1859 text)
Joel, whose name, according to S. Jerom, signifies the Lord God, (or, as others say, the coming down of God) prophesied about the same time in the kingdom of Juda as Osee did in the kingdom of Israel. He foretells, under figures, the great evils that were coming upon the people for their sins; earnestly exhorts them to repentance, and comforts them with the promise of a teacher of justice, viz. Christ Jesus, our Lord, and of the coming down of his Holy Spirit (Ch). upon the hundred and twenty faithful assembled in Sion. He describes the land of the twelve tribes made desolate, and the people cast off. S. Jer. ad Paulin. — Yet he speaks chiefly of the kingdom of Juda, and mentions the house of God, sacrifices, &c. (Worthington) — S. Jerom infers from his being placed after Osee, without any fresh title, (Calmet) that he lived in that order of time. (Worthington) — But this rule is not general, as Jonas lived before Amos; and Septuagint observe not the same disposition of the prophets as we do. The exact time of the famine, when Joel prophesied, cannot be ascertained. It seems he addressed the people in autumn, when a second year's famine was apprehended. He paints every thing with great force and beauty of style. (Calmet)
