Douay: Jonas · 4 chapters

At a glance

Author

Jewish tradition took Jonas son of Amathi, the prophet of Jeroboam II's time (4 Kings 14:25), as the author. The 1913 article notes that the book nowhere claims to be by him, and that most modern scholars date its writing much later.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Jonas”↗Challoner, Introduction↗

Date

Jonas himself lived in the 8th century B.C., and the 1913 article places the story in a period of Assyrian decline (about 770-745 B.C.); most modern scholars, it reports, date the book's composition probably to the 5th century B.C.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Jonas”↗

Setting

Joppe, the sea, and Ninive, 'the great city' of the Assyrians, Israel's enemy, to which Jonas is sent to preach repentance; Challoner notes that Jonas came from Geth-Opher in Galilee.

About this book

Audience

Israel, taught through a reluctant prophet that God's mercy reaches even the Gentiles who repent.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Jonas”↗Challoner, Introduction↗

Purpose

The 1913 article says the writer told a story of glory to the God of Israel and downfall to the gods of Ninive. Challoner and Haydock stress that Jonas, the only prophet sent to preach to the Gentiles, prefigured in his own person the death and resurrection of Christ.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Jonas”↗Challoner, Introduction↗Haydock (1859), Preface↗

Key themes

  • God's call, and the prophet's flight from it.
  • Three days in the fish: the sign of Jonas.
  • The repentance of Ninive.
  • God's mercy for the Gentiles, and the prophet's anger at it (4).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Jonas”↗Challoner, Introduction↗Matt 12:39-41

Structure

Jonas's flight and the storm (1); his prayer from the belly of the fish (2); his preaching and Ninive's repentance (3); and his complaint, answered by God's lesson of the ivy (4).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Jonas”↗

How the Church reads it

Jesus himself gives 'the sign of Jonas the prophet': as Jonas was three days and three nights in the fish, so the Son of Man would be in the heart of the earth, and the men of Ninive will rise in judgment because they repented (Matt 12:39-41; Luke 11:29-32). The 1913 article says that to the Fathers Jonas was a type of the Messias, and that St. Jerome, St. Cyril and Theophilus explain the type in detail; Challoner and Haydock add that he prefigured Christ's death and resurrection and the call of the Gentiles.

Matt 12:39-41; Luke 11:29-32Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Jonas”↗Challoner, Introduction↗Haydock (1859), Preface↗

In the canon

One of the twelve minor prophets in Trent's list (Session IV) and in the Catechism's (CCC 120); Haydock notes that Sirach already speaks of 'the twelve' as a collection (Ecclesiasticus 49:12 Douay).

The old texts and the sources

Haydock's preface (1859 text)
Jonas prophesied in the reign of Jeroboam II. as we learn from 4 K. xiv. 25. to whom also he foretold his success in restoring all the borders of Israel. He was of Geth — Opher, in the tribe of Zabulon, and consequently of Galilee; which confutes that assertion of the Pharisees, (John vii, 52.) that no prophet ever arose out of Galilee. He prophesied and prefigured in his own person the death and resurrection of Christ, and was the only one among the prophets who was sent to preach to the Gentiles. (Challoner) — The most incredible mystery in our religion, and the vocation of the Gentiles, are thus insinuated. (Calmet) — The latter shall be saved if they repent, like Ninive. (Worthington) — Paine's supposition, that this book was written by a pagan "to satirise the malignant character of a predicting priest," requires no refutation. H. Watson.
Challoner's introduction (1749–52)
JONAS prophesied in the reign of JEREBOAM the second: as we learn from 4 Kings 14.25. To whom also he foretold his success in restoring all the borders of Israel. He was of GETH OPHER in the tribe of ZABULON, and consequently of GALILEE: which confutes that assertion of the Pharisees, John 7.52, that no prophet ever rose out of GALILEE. He prophesied and prefigured in his own person the death and resurrection of CHRIST: and was the only one among the prophets that was sent to preach to the Gentiles.
