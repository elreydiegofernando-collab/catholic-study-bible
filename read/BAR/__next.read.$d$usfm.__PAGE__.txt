(Challoner) — The frequent Hebrew idioms shew it to have been originally in that language. Baruch wrote by inspiration of the Holy Ghost the letter comprising the five first chapters, which he carried to the Jews from their brethren at Babylon. The martyrologies place his death, Dec. 28. The sixth chapter contains a letter of Jeremias, to which allusion is clearly made 2 Mac. ii. 2. The Church still recites the works of Baruch under the name of Jeremias. Sab. Pent. proph. 6. (Calmet) — Many Fathers did so formerly, though they doubted not but Baruch was the author. See S. Iræn. v. 35. S. Aug. de Civ. Dei. xviii. 33. &c. Others, with Origen (Prin. ii. 3.) specify the writer; and the Councils of Laodicea, Florence, and Trent, decide that it is canonical. S. Jerom alleged it not against the Jews, as they denied its authority. (Worthington) — See Jer. xxxii. 44. (Haydock) — In his preface on Jeremias he testifies that "it contains many things relating to Christ and the latter times." (Worthington) — Grotius in vain attempts to represent some parts as interpolated (C. iii. 38. &c. C.) and L.
