Zephaniah

Douay: Sophonias · 3 chapters

At a glance

Author

Sophonias, whose genealogy the title traces back four generations, more than for any other prophet; the 1913 article says even radical critics accept that the Sophonias of the title is the author. Challoner gives St. Jerome's meanings of his name, 'the watchman of the Lord' or 'the hidden of the Lord', and the more general opinion that he was of the tribe of Simeon, from Sarabatha.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Sophonias”↗Challoner, Introduction↗

Date

Dated by the title to the reign of Josias (641-611 B.C.), early in it according to Challoner; the 1913 article finds the idolatry described fits the first years of Josias, before his reform.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Sophonias”↗Challoner, Introduction↗

Setting

Jerusalem after the reigns of Manasses and Amon, when the worship of Baal and of the heavenly bodies on the rooftops had spread in the holy city.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Sophonias”↗

About this book

Audience

Juda and Jerusalem, especially the nobles, judges, prophets and priests, with the surrounding nations also summoned to judgment.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Sophonias”↗

Purpose

To announce the day of the Lord as a judgment on Juda and on all nations, calling the humble to seek the Lord, and then to promise a purified remnant and a future in which all the world turns to one God.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Sophonias”↗Challoner, Introduction↗

Key themes

  • The day of the Lord, a day of wrath (1:14-18).
  • 'Seek the Lord, all ye meek of the earth' (2:3).
  • Judgment on the nations in all four directions (2:4-15).
  • A poor and humble remnant who trust in the Lord (3:12).
  • 'Give praise, O daughter of Sion' (3:14).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Sophonias”↗CCC 711↗CCC 716↗CCC 722↗

Structure

The 1913 article finds the day of the Lord against Juda (1:2-2:3), judgment on the nations (2:4-15), a special threat to Jerusalem (3:1-8), and a prophecy of consolation (3:9-20); it calls the opening an Old Testament 'Dies irae'.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Sophonias”↗

How the Church reads it

The Catechism finds in Sophonias's 'poor and humble' remnant the people who awaited the consolation of Israel (CCC 711, 716), and hears in his call to the daughter of Sion to rejoice the greeting fulfilled in Mary, in whom God dwells (CCC 722, 2676). The 1913 article calls his book, for its universal judgment and redemption, the 'Catholic Epistle' of the Old Testament.

CCC 711↗CCC 716↗CCC 722↗CCC 2676↗Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Sophonias”↗

In the canon

One of the twelve minor prophets in Trent's list (Session IV) and in the Catechism's (CCC 120); Haydock notes that Sirach already speaks of 'the twelve' as a collection (Ecclesiasticus 49:12 Douay).

The old texts and the sources

Haydock's preface (1859 text)
Sophonias, whose name, saith S. Jerom, signifies "the watchman of the Lord," or "the hidden of the Lord," prophesied in the beginning of the reign of Josias. He was a native of Sarabatha, and of the tribe of Simeon, according to the more general opinion. He prophesied the punishments of the Jews, for their idolatry and other crimes; also the punishments that were to come on divers nations; the coming of Christ, the conversion of the Gentiles, the blindness of the Jews, and their conversion towards the end of the world. (Challoner) — Some editions read, Ezechias. (Haydock) — But this opinion is not well grounded no more than that of the Jews, who assert (Calmet) that all the ancestors mentioned by the prophets were endued with the prophetic spirit, for which reason Amos specifies none, as he was not the son of a prophet. C. vii. 14. (St. Jerome) — Sophonias appeared a little before Jeremias, Ezec. Bar. and Daniel, foretelling the captivity and return of the two tribes, the destruction of various nations, the conversion of the Gentiles, and of the Jews also towards the end of the world. (Worthington) — Many of the promises regard only the Christian Church. (Calmet)
