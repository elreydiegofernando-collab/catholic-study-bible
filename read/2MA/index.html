2 Maccabees†

Douay: 2 Machabees · 15 chapters · deuterocanonical

At a glance

Author

Challoner and the 1913 article agree that it is a short abstract of the five books of Jason of Cyrene (2:24 and following), made by an unknown author who is not the author of 1 Machabees. He wrote in Greek, and two letters from the Jews of Jerusalem to those of Egypt open the book.

Challoner, Introduction↗Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Books of Machabees”, The Second Book of Machabees, Author and Date↗Haydock (1859), Preface↗

Date

The 1913 article says the abridgment cannot be earlier than the first letter, dated 124 B.C., and very probably went with it; Jason's own work ended soon after Nicanor's death in 161 B.C. It notes great divergence over the exact date.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Books of Machabees”, The Second Book of Machabees, Author and Date↗

Setting

Jerusalem and Judea from Heliodorus's attempt on the Temple treasury under Seleucus IV to the defeat of Nicanor: about fifteen years, 176-161 B.C.

About this book

Audience

The 1913 article thinks it was meant especially for the Jews of the Dispersion in Egypt, where a rival temple had been built at Leontopolis.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Books of Machabees”, The Second Book of Machabees, Object and Character↗

Purpose

The 1913 article says the author wrote for instruction and edification, first to exalt the Temple of Jerusalem as the center of worship, and second to urge fidelity to the Law, since God does not abandon his people unless they abandon him. Hence he comments freely and stresses God's interventions on their behalf.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Books of Machabees”, The Second Book of Machabees, Object and Character↗

Key themes

  • The holiness of the Temple, protected and avenged by God.
  • Martyrdom: Eleazar and the mother with her seven sons (6-7).
  • Hope in the resurrection of the dead.
  • God's creation of all things out of nothing (7:28).
  • Prayer and sacrifice for the dead (12:43-46).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Books of Machabees”, The Second Book of Machabees, Object and Character↗CCC 992↗CCC 297↗CCC 958↗CCC 1032↗

Structure

Two letters to the Jews of Egypt (1:1-2:19); the author's preface (2:20-33); Heliodorus and the troubles under Seleucus IV (3:1-4:6); the reign of Antiochus Epiphanes, with the martyrs and the Temple's rededication (4:7-10:9); the wars under Antiochus Eupator and Demetrius I to Nicanor's defeat (10:10-15:37); and a short epilogue (15:38-40).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Books of Machabees”, The Second Book of Machabees, Contents↗

How the Church reads it

The Catechism cites Judas Machabeus's offering for the dead (12:46) as the scriptural basis of the Church's practice of praying for the dead (CCC 958, 1032). It hears in the Machabean martyrs a confession of faith in the resurrection (CCC 992), and in the mother's words to her sons a witness to creation out of nothing (CCC 297).

In the canon

A deuterocanonical book, received with 1 Machabees by the Church and declared canonical at Florence and Trent, as Challoner notes (Trent, Session IV; CCC 120).

The old texts and the sources

Haydock's preface (1859 text)
This Second Book of Machabees is not a continuation of the history contained in the First; nor does it come down so low as the First does, but relates many of the same facts more at large, and adds other remarkable particulars, omitted in the First Book, relating to the state of the Jews as well before as under the persecution of Antiochus. The author, who is not the same with that of the First Book, has given (as we learn from C. ii. 20. &c.) a short abstract of what Jason, of Cyrene, had written in the five volumes, concerning Judas and his brethren. He wrote in Greek, and begins with two letters, sent by the Jews of Jerusalem to their brethren in Egypt. (Challoner) — Hence the whole book has been considered by some as an epistle. Cotel. Can. Ap. p. 338. — But it is easy to distinguish the letter from the history, (Calmet) to which a preface is prefixed. C. ii. 20. Yet the Alex. copy has at the end, "A letter concerning the acts of Judas Mach." (Haydock) — The appendix of two epistles was added to the First Book by him who wrote the second, (Worthington) abridging the work of Jason. (Haydock)
Challoner's introduction (1749–52)
This second book of MACHABEES is not a continuation of the history contained in the first: nor does is come down so low as the first does: but relates many of the same facts more at large, and adds other remarkable particulars, omitted in the first book, relating to the state of the Jews, as well before as under the persecution of ANTIOCHUS. The author, who is not the same with that of the first book, has given (as we learn from chap. 2.20, etc.) a short abstract of what JASON of Cyrene had written in the five volumes, concerning JUDAS and his brethren. He wrote in Greek, and begins with two letters, sent by the Jews of Jerusalem to their brethren in Egypt.
