Haggai

Douay: Aggeus · 2 chapters

At a glance

Author

Aggeus, of whom little is known; the 1913 article says Jewish traditions about him (born in Chaldea, an angel in human form, a member of the Great Synagogue, living until Alexander or even Christ) deserve little credence. Challoner and Haydock say he returned from Babylon in the first year of Cyrus and may have been born in captivity.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Aggeus (Haggai)”, 1. Name and Personal Life↗Challoner, Introduction↗Haydock (1859), Preface↗

Date

His four dated messages fall in the second year of Darius I, 520 B.C., between the sixth and ninth months.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Aggeus (Haggai)”, 3. The Prophecies↗Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Aggeus (Haggai)”, 1. Name and Personal Life↗

Setting

Jerusalem sixteen years after the first return from exile, when the rebuilding of the Temple, begun and then stopped by Samaritan opposition, had been abandoned while the people built their own fine houses.

About this book

Audience

Zorobabel the governor, Jesus the high priest, and the people of Juda.

Challoner, Introduction↗Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Aggeus (Haggai)”, 3. The Prophecies↗

Purpose

To rouse the people to finish the Temple, reading a recent drought as God's punishment for their neglect; Challoner adds that the prophet assured them this second temple would be more glorious than the first because the Messias would honor it with his presence.

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Aggeus (Haggai)”, 3. The Prophecies↗Challoner, Introduction↗

Key themes

  • God's house before our own houses (1).
  • Courage to build: 'I am with you, saith the Lord' (1:13; 2:5).
  • The latter glory of the second Temple (2:10 Douay).
  • 'The desired of all nations shall come' (2:8 Douay).
  • Zorobabel as God's chosen signet (2:24 Douay).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Aggeus (Haggai)”, 3. The Prophecies↗Challoner, Introduction↗

Structure

The 1913 article finds four messages, each headed by its date: a call to rebuild (1), the promise of the Temple's future glory (2:1-10 Douay), a lesson on clean and unclean with a promise of blessing (2:11-20), and a promise to Zorobabel (2:21-24).

Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), “Aggeus (Haggai)”, 3. The Prophecies↗

How the Church reads it

Challoner reads the Temple's greater glory as the Messias's presence in it, and the whole as showing how far the Church of the New Testament would excel that of the Old; Haydock (citing Worthington) adds that 'the glory of the Catholic Church hence appears'. Hebrews 12:26-27 quotes the promise that God will once more shake heaven and earth (2:7 Douay, 2:6 modern).

Challoner, Introduction↗Haydock (1859), Preface↗Heb 12:26-27

In the canon

One of the twelve minor prophets in Trent's list (Session IV) and in the Catechism's (CCC 120); Haydock notes that Sirach already speaks of 'the twelve' as a collection (Ecclesiasticus 49:12 Douay).

The old texts and the sources

Haydock's preface (1859 text)
Aggeus was one of those that returned from the captivity of Babylon, in the first year of the reign of king Cyrus. He was sent by the Lord in the second year of the reign of Darius, the son of Hystaspes, to exhort Zorobabel, the prince of Juda, and Jesus, the high priest, to the building of the temple; which they had begun, but left off again through opposition of the Samaritans. In consequence of this exhortation, they proceeded in the building, and finished the temple. And the prophet was commissioned by the Lord to assure them that this second temple should be more glorious than the former, because the Messias should honour it with his presence; signifying, withal, how much the Church of the new testament should excel that of the old testament. (Challoner) — The glory of the Catholic Church hence appears. (Worthington) — We know little of the life of Aggeus. It is thought that he was born in captivity. (Calmet) — He came into Judea eighteen years after its termination, (Worthington) in the second year of Hystaspes, when the seventy years of the temple's desolation ended. Zac. i. 12. and 1 Esd. v. The people had courage to obey the word of the prophets rather than the king's edict. Aggeus means feasting, (St. Jerome) or pleasant. He brings joyful tidings, after rebuking the people for preferring their own convenience before the house of God. (Haydock)
